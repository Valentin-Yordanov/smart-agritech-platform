# Agronomic Guide: Tomato Fruit Problems (Blossom End Rot, Sunscald, Fruit Borer, Rots)

## Blossom End Rot (physiological, calcium-related)
- **Cause:** Not an infection. Calcium does not reach the growing fruit, usually because of uneven watering, root damage, high salinity or excess nitrogen/ammonium.
- **Symptoms:** A water-soaked spot at the blossom end that turns into a dark brown to black, leathery, sunken patch.
- **Management:** Water evenly (drip irrigation and mulch), avoid excess nitrogen, keep soil pH around 6.2 to 6.8 and add calcium (lime or gypsum) if the soil test shows a shortage. Remove affected fruit; it will not recover.

## Sunscald
- **Cause:** Fruit exposed to strong sun after leaves are lost (disease, pruning) or during heat waves.
- **Symptoms:** White or yellow papery patches on the side facing the sun; may be invaded by mould later.
- **Management:** Keep enough leaf cover, avoid over-pruning, use shade nets in very hot periods, and control leaf diseases.

## Tomato Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera)
- **Symptoms:** Round holes in fruit, often near the stem, with caterpillar frass; fruit rots afterwards.
- **Management:** Pheromone traps to monitor, remove and destroy bored fruit, trap crops (e.g. marigold), Bt or NPV biopesticides, and targeted insecticides (spinosad, chlorantraniliprole, emamectin benzoate) against young larvae.

## Fruit Rots (Rotten Fruit)
- **Causes:** Fungi (*Botrytis* grey mould, *Alternaria*, anthracnose) and bacteria (soft rot) entering through wounds, cracks or the stem end.
- **Management:** Harvest gently and at the right time, avoid fruit contact with soil (staking, mulch), remove rotten fruit from the field and storage, keep storage cool and dry, and control fruit-feeding insects that create wounds.
