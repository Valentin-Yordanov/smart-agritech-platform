# Agronomic Guide: Powdery Mildew (Erysiphales)

## Overview & Pathology
Powdery mildew is a common fungal disease affecting a vast spectrum of horticultural crops, including cucurbits (cucumber, melon, squash), Solanaceae (tomato, pepper), apples, and grapes. It is caused by various species of obligate biotrophic fungi within the order Erysiphales (e.g., *Podosphaera xanthii*, *Golovinomyces cichoracearum*, *Leveillula taurica*).

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature Range:** Warm daytime temperatures between 20°C and 28°C (68°F to 82°F). High temperatures (>35°C) inhibit spore germination.
- **Moisture Conditions:** Unlike downy mildew, powdery mildew does *not* require free standing water on foliage. In fact, heavy rainfall washes away conidia. It thrives under high ambient relative humidity (70-90%) combined with shaded, poor air circulation environments.
- **Microclimate:** Dense plant canopies with excess nitrogen fertilization promote lush, susceptible succulent growth.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Foliar Surface Growth:** Characteristic talcum-powder or white-to-grayish powdery fungal patches on both upper and lower leaf surfaces, petioles, and younger stems.
- **Senescence & Chlorosis:** Underlying tissue turns yellow (chlorotic), curls upward, turns brown (necrotic), and drops prematurely.
- **Internal Mildew (*Leveillula taurica* on Pepper/Tomato):** Unlike classic surface mildews, *L. taurica* produces diffuse, bright yellow chlorotic angular lesions on the upper leaf surface, with sparse powdery white growth hidden exclusively on the underside.

## Integrated Pest & Disease Management (IPM)

### Cultural & Physical Controls
1. **Airflow & Canopy Thinning:** Prune dense foliage, trellis climbing crops, and maintain wide plant row spacing to optimize sunlight penetration and wind movement.
2. **Balanced Nutrition:** Avoid excessive nitrate-nitrogen fertilizers which induce succulent vegetative growth vulnerable to hyphal penetration. Maintain balanced potassium (K) and calcium (Ca) nutrition to strengthen plant cell walls.
3. **Resistant Hybrids:** Utilize cultivars bred with specific powdery mildew resistance genes (e.g., PMR cucurbit varieties).

### Organic & Biorational Controls
- **Sulfur Formulations:** Wettable sulfur or micronized sulfur dust is one of the most effective organic treatments. Warning: Do not apply sulfur when temperatures exceed 32°C (90°F) or within 14 days of an oil spray to prevent severe foliar phytotoxicity/scorch.
- **Potassium Bicarbonate (KHCO3):** Contact salt that alters leaf surface pH and osmotic pressure, rapidly collapsing fungal hyphae and spores.
- **Horticultural Oils & Plant Extracts:** Horticultural mineral oils (1%), neem oil (azadirachtin), or tea tree oil (reynoutria extract) coat leaf cuticle and prevent conidia attachment.
- **Biological Agents:** Biofungicides containing *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* or *Ampelomyces quisqualis* (a hyperparasite of powdery mildew).

### Conventional Chemical Controls
- **Fungicide Rotation:** Myclobutanil / Tebuconazole (DMI / FRAC 3), Trifloxystrobin / Azoxystrobin (QoI / FRAC 11), or Cyflufenamid (FRAC U06). Alternate chemical groups each application to prevent rapid development of resistant strains.
- **Spray Coverage:** Ensure complete canopy penetration, especially underside foliage and inner stems.
