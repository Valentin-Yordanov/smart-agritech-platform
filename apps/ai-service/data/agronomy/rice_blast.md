# Agronomic Guide: Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae)

## Overview & Pathology
Rice blast, caused by the fungus *Magnaporthe oryzae* (anamorph *Pyricularia oryzae*), is the most damaging fungal disease of rice worldwide. It attacks leaves (leaf blast), the stem nodes (node blast) and the panicle neck (neck blast). Neck blast is the most costly form because it cuts the supply to the grain and causes white, empty panicles.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature:** 24°C to 28°C is optimal for infection; cool nights favour dew formation.
- **Moisture:** Leaf wetness of 10 hours or more, high humidity (>90%) and frequent light rain.
- **Crop factors:** Excess or late nitrogen, upland or aerobic rice, drought stress and dense planting all raise risk.
- **Spread:** Airborne conidia from infected stubble, straw, weeds and seed.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Leaf blast:** Spindle- or diamond-shaped lesions with grey or whitish centres and brown to reddish-brown margins.
- **Node blast:** Blackened nodes that break easily.
- **Neck blast:** Grey-brown rot at the base of the panicle; panicles turn white and stay upright with empty grains.
- **Collar rot:** Brown lesions at the junction of leaf blade and sheath, often killing the whole leaf.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. Plant resistant or tolerant varieties suited to the region.
2. Use certified, clean seed and treat seed with a fungicide where blast is common.
3. Split nitrogen into several doses; avoid heavy top-dressing after panicle initiation.
4. Keep fields flooded where possible; drought stress increases blast.
5. Destroy infected stubble and straw and control grassy weeds that host the fungus.

### Chemical Control
- **Tricyclazole** (melanin biosynthesis inhibitor) as a preventive spray at late tillering and at booting/heading.
- **Azoxystrobin**, **isoprothiolane** or a **triazole** (tebuconazole, propiconazole) when lesions are spreading.
- Protect the neck with one spray at booting and one at 50% heading in high-risk seasons.
- Rotate fungicide groups to slow resistance.
