# Agronomic Guide: Fall Armyworm on Maize (Spodoptera frugiperda)

## Overview
Fall armyworm is a moth whose caterpillars feed on maize, sorghum, rice and many other crops. Since 2016 it has spread across Africa and Asia. Moths fly long distances, so outbreaks can appear suddenly.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- Small "windowpane" patches on young leaves where young larvae scrape the surface.
- Ragged holes and torn leaves in the whorl, with moist sawdust-like frass (droppings).
- Larvae with an inverted pale "Y" on the head and four dark spots in a square on the second-to-last body segment.
- Later damage to tassels and ears.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

### Scouting
- Check 20 plants in a W-shaped walk across the field twice a week during the first 6 weeks.
- Act when about 20% of plants show fresh damage at the whorl stage.

### Cultural & Biological Control
1. Plant early and at the same time as neighbours to avoid peak moth flights.
2. Intercrop with legumes (e.g. push-pull with *Desmodium* and border Napier grass).
3. Conserve natural enemies (parasitic wasps, earwigs, ants) by avoiding unnecessary broad-spectrum sprays.
4. Biopesticides: *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt), *Metarhizium* or *Beauveria* fungi, and SfMNPV virus products.

### Chemical Control
- Target young larvae in the whorl in the early morning or evening.
- Effective actives include emamectin benzoate, spinetoram and chlorantraniliprole.
- Rotate insecticide groups (IRAC codes) to slow resistance.
