# Agronomic Guide: Banana Fusarium Wilt (Panama Disease) and Black Sigatoka

## Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense)

### Overview
Fusarium wilt is a soil-borne fungal disease that blocks the water-conducting tissue of the banana plant. Tropical Race 4 (TR4) attacks Cavendish and many other varieties. The fungus survives in soil for decades, so infested land cannot be cleaned.

### Symptoms
- Yellowing of older leaves, starting at the margins and moving inward.
- Leaves collapse at the petiole and hang around the pseudostem like a skirt.
- Splitting of the pseudostem base.
- Cutting the pseudostem shows reddish-brown to black streaks in the vascular tissue.

### Management
1. No fungicide cures the disease. Destroy infected plants in place (do not move soil or plant material).
2. Quarantine infected areas: clean boots, tools and vehicles, and restrict movement.
3. Plant tissue-culture plantlets from certified nurseries.
4. Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available.
5. Improve drainage and avoid soil compaction.

## Black Sigatoka (Pseudocercospora fijiensis)

### Overview
Black Sigatoka is an airborne leaf-spot disease that destroys leaf area and causes bunches to ripen early and be smaller. It thrives in warm, wet conditions.

### Symptoms
- Small reddish-brown streaks on the underside of young leaves.
- Streaks enlarge into dark brown to black spots with grey centres and yellow halos.
- Spots join, killing large parts of the leaf.

### Management
1. Remove and destroy spotted leaves (de-leafing) every 1 to 2 weeks.
2. Keep good drainage and plant spacing to lower humidity.
3. Rotate protectant (mancozeb, chlorothalonil) with systemic fungicides (triazoles, strobilurins, SDHIs), often with mineral oil.
4. Limit systemic sprays per season to slow resistance.
