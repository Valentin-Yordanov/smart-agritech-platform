# Agronomic Guide: Coffee Leaf Rust (Hemileia vastatrix)

## Overview & Pathology
Coffee leaf rust, caused by the fungus *Hemileia vastatrix*, is the most important disease of arabica coffee. Heavy infection causes leaf drop, weak branches and lower yields in the following season.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature:** 21°C to 25°C.
- **Moisture:** Liquid water on leaves for spore germination; epidemics follow the rainy season.
- **Crop factors:** Heavy crop load, poor nutrition and old, susceptible varieties.
- **Spread:** Wind and rain splash carry spores between leaves and plants.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- Pale yellow spots on the upper leaf surface.
- Bright orange powdery pustules on the underside of the leaf, under the yellow spots.
- Older lesions turn brown and dry in the centre.
- Severe premature leaf drop.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. Plant rust-resistant varieties where suitable for the region.
2. Prune to open the canopy and remove unproductive stems.
3. Fertilise in balance with crop load; well-fed plants resist rust better.
4. Manage shade: moderate shade can reduce disease pressure.

### Chemical Control
- **Copper fungicides** (copper hydroxide or oxychloride) as protectants before and early in the rainy season.
- **Triazoles** (cyproconazole, tebuconazole) or **strobilurins** where infection is established.
- Time sprays to the start of the rainy season and repeat according to label intervals.
