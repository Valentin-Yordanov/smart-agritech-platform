# Agronomic Guide: Rice Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)

## Overview & Pathology
Bacterial leaf blight (BLB) is caused by *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae*. It is one of the most serious rice diseases in irrigated and rainfed lowlands of Asia and Africa. Early infection of seedlings (kresek) can kill whole hills.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature:** 25°C to 34°C.
- **Moisture:** Typhoons, storms and flooding spread the bacteria and wound the leaves.
- **Crop factors:** High nitrogen, close spacing, deep standing water and clipping seedlings at transplanting.
- **Spread:** Irrigation water, rain splash, infected stubble and weeds.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- Water-soaked stripes starting at the leaf tip or margins that turn yellow, then straw-white.
- Lesions have wavy margins and run down the leaf blade.
- Milky or yellow bacterial ooze droplets on young lesions early in the morning.
- **Kresek:** Seedlings wilt, roll up and die 1 to 3 weeks after transplanting.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. Grow resistant varieties (carrying genes such as Xa4, xa5, Xa21).
2. Use clean seed and avoid clipping seedling leaf tips at transplanting.
3. Balance nitrogen with potassium; avoid excess nitrogen.
4. Drain fields intermittently and avoid water flowing from infected to healthy fields.
5. Plough in or remove infected stubble and weed hosts after harvest.

### Chemical Control
- No spray cures BLB. Copper-based bactericides can slow spread early but have limited effect.
- Where approved, seed treatment and copper sprays at the first symptoms can reduce secondary spread.
