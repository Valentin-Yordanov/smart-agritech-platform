# Agronomic Guide: Potato & Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

## Overview & Pathology
Late blight, caused by the oomycete *Phytophthora infestans*, is an aggressive, destructive water mold responsible for catastrophic losses in potato (*Solanum tuberosum*) and tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*). Because it spreads via windborne sporangia over miles, an entire field can be defoliated within 7 to 14 days under conducive microclimates.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature Range:** Cool to moderate temperatures: 10°C to 18°C (50°F to 64°F) for sporangia germination and zoospore release; 16°C to 21°C for mycelial colonization.
- **Moisture Conditions:** High relative humidity (>90%) and free moisture (fog, dew, rainfall, sprinkler irrigation) on leaf surfaces for at least 8 to 10 hours.
- **Vectors & Survival:** Overwinters primarily in infected seed tubers, cull piles, and volunteer potato sprouts.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Foliar Lesions:** Water-soaked, irregularly shaped, pale green to brown lesions rapidly expanding into large, dark brown to purplish-black necrotic areas.
- **Underside Sporulation:** Under high humidity, a delicate white cottony or downy mildew growth (sporangiophores) appears on the lower leaf surface bordering the lesion margin.
- **Stem & Petiole Cankers:** Dark brown to black greasy-looking necrotic lesions along stems, rendering branches brittle and prone to breakage.
- **Tuber Symptoms:** Copper-brown to reddish-purple dry granular rot extending 5 to 15 mm into the flesh beneath the tuber skin, often followed by secondary bacterial soft rot (*Pectobacterium*).

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. **Certified Seed Tubers:** Plant exclusively certified disease-free seed potatoes. Never plant cull or uncertified garden tubers.
2. **Cull Pile Elimination:** Bury, freeze-desiccate, or destroy all cull piles, volunteer sprouts, and compost debris before planting season.
3. **Moisture Control & Spacing:** Avoid sprinkler irrigation in late afternoons. Space rows to facilitate rapid leaf drying and aerodynamic airflow.
4. **Vine Killing (Potato):** Terminate potato foliage (chemical desiccation or mechanical flailing) 2 to 3 weeks before harvest to prevent tuber contact with active foliar sporangia during lifting.

### Organic & Bio-Protective Controls
- **Fixed Copper Fungicides:** Copper octanoate, basic copper sulfate, or copper hydroxide applied in proactive preventative blocks when late blight forecasting models (e.g., Blitecast) signal elevated risk.
- **Bio-inoculants:** *Bacillus subtilis* and *Trichoderma harzianum* for root and crown protection, though chemical control is essential under high epidemic pressure.

### Targeted Chemical Control (Conventional)
- **Protectant Regimes:** Mancozeb, Chlorothalonil, or Fluazinam prior to disease detection to establish protective surface barriers.
- **Oomycete-Specific Systemics:** Mandipropamid (FRAC 40), Cymoxanil (FRAC 27), Propamocarb (FRAC 28), or Metalaxyl/Mefenoxam (FRAC 4, monitor for regional resistance strains like US-23).
- **Resistance Management:** Alternate distinct FRAC mode-of-action groups every spray cycle. Always adhere to stated Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) and buffer zones.
