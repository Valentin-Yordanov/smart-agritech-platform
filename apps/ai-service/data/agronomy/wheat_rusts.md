# Agronomic Guide: Wheat Rusts (Yellow/Stripe, Brown/Leaf and Stem Rust)

## Overview & Pathology
Three *Puccinia* fungi cause the wheat rusts:
- **Yellow (stripe) rust**, *Puccinia striiformis* f. sp. *tritici*.
- **Brown (leaf) rust**, *Puccinia triticina*.
- **Black (stem) rust**, *Puccinia graminis* f. sp. *tritici*.

Rust spores travel hundreds of kilometres on the wind, so epidemics can develop quickly over whole regions. New races regularly overcome resistant varieties.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Yellow rust:** Cool weather, 10°C to 15°C, with dew or light rain; often the first rust of spring.
- **Brown rust:** 15°C to 25°C with leaf wetness of a few hours.
- **Stem rust:** Warm, 20°C to 30°C, later in the season.
- **Survival:** Volunteer wheat and green bridge crops between seasons.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Yellow rust:** Small yellow-orange pustules arranged in stripes between leaf veins; heavily infected leaves dry out early.
- **Brown rust:** Round to oval orange-brown pustules scattered on the upper leaf surface, not in stripes.
- **Stem rust:** Large, elongated brick-red pustules on stems and leaf sheaths that tear the epidermis; turn black late in the season.
- Rubbing a pustule leaves rust-coloured powder (spores) on the finger.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. Grow varieties with current resistance ratings for your region; avoid growing a single variety on a large area.
2. Remove volunteer wheat and grass hosts to break the green bridge.
3. Avoid very early sowing in yellow-rust areas and excessive nitrogen.
4. Scout fields weekly from tillering; check the upper three leaves.

### Chemical Control
- **Triazoles** (tebuconazole, propiconazole, prothioconazole) at the first pustules.
- **Strobilurins** (azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin) mixed with a triazole for longer protection.
- The key timing is protecting the flag leaf (growth stage 37 to 39) when disease is present.
- Rotate or mix fungicide groups.
