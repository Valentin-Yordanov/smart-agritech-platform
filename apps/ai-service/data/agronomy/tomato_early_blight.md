# Agronomic Guide: Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

## Overview & Pathology
Early blight is a pervasive fungal disease caused primarily by *Alternaria solani* (and secondarily *Alternaria tomatophila*) affecting Solanaceae crops, especially tomatoes (*Solanum lycopersicum*). It attacks foliage, stems, and fruit throughout all vegetative and reproductive stages, often causing severe defoliation and yield collapse if unmanaged.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Temperature Range:** Optimal development occurs at moderate to warm temperatures between 24°C and 29°C (75°F to 85°F).
- **Moisture Conditions:** Fungal conidia germinate rapidly under prolonged leaf wetness (>2 hours) and ambient relative humidity exceeding 85%. Alternate dry and wet cycles accelerate conidial dispersion by wind and splashing irrigation.
- **Overwintering:** Pathogen survives in infected solanaceous crop debris, volunteer plants, and solanaceous weeds (e.g., black nightshade).

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Foliar Lesions:** Lower, older leaves display initial dark brown to black necrotic spots. As lesions expand (6-12 mm), they develop characteristic concentric concentric rings, producing a distinct "bullseye" or target-board pattern surrounded by chlorotic (yellow) halos.
- **Stem Lesions:** Collar rot on seedlings at the soil line; elongated, dark brown, sunken lesions on mature stems.
- **Fruit Symptoms:** Leathery, sunken, dark brown to black lesions near the calyx attachment, often accompanied by concentric ring patterns.

## Integrated Pest & Disease Management (IPM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. **Crop Rotation:** Implement a strict 3- to 4-year crop rotation schedule excluding solanaceous crops (potato, eggplant, pepper, tomato).
2. **Canopy & Water Management:** Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation. Employ drip irrigation to keep foliage dry. Prune lower sucker shoots and leaves up to 30 cm above ground level to enhance air circulation and reduce splash inoculation.
3. **Resistant Cultivars:** Select cultivars with documented tolerance or resistance (e.g., Defiant PhR, Mountain Merit, Jasper).
4. **Sanitation & Mulching:** Apply clean organic straw or polyethylene mulch around the base of plants to prevent rain-splash transmission of soil-borne spores. Promptly rogue and destroy heavily infested crop residues.

### Biological & Organic Controls
- **Copper-based Formulations:** Copper hydroxide or copper octanoate (bordeaux mixture) applied preventively every 7-10 days under warm, humid conditions.
- **Microbial Antagonists:** Foliar biofungicides containing *Bacillus subtilis* (strain QST 713) or *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* to colonize leaf surfaces and suppress fungal sporulation.
- **Botanical Sprays:** Cold-pressed neem oil (azadirachtin) or potassium bicarbonate sprays to disrupt fungal cell membrane osmolarity.

### Chemical Fungicide Regimes
- **Preventive / Contact:** Chlorothalonil or Mancozeb applied prior to canopy closure or at first appearance of lower leaf lesions.
- **Curative / Translaminar Systemic:** Azoxystrobin, Pyraclostrobin (strobilurins / FRAC 11), or Difenoconazole / Boscalid (FRAC 3 / FRAC 7). Always rotate chemical classes with different FRAC codes to mitigate fungicide resistance development.
- **Safety & Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI):** Adhere strictly to the product label's PHI (typically 1 to 7 days for azoxystrobin/copper) and re-entry intervals (REI).
