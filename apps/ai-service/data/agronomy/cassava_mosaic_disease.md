# Agronomic Guide: Cassava Mosaic Disease (Cassava Mosaic Begomoviruses)

## Overview & Pathology
Cassava mosaic disease (CMD) is caused by several begomoviruses (for example *African cassava mosaic virus* and *East African cassava mosaic virus*). It is the most widespread cassava disease in Africa and can reduce root yield by 20% to 90%. The virus spreads through infected stem cuttings and the whitefly *Bemisia tabaci*.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- **Main source:** Planting cuttings taken from infected plants.
- **Vector:** High whitefly populations in warm, dry periods spread the virus between plants.
- **Risk factors:** Susceptible local varieties and mixed fields with infected plants.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- Yellow or pale green mosaic patches on leaves.
- Leaflets distorted, twisted, reduced in size and puckered.
- Stunted plants with fewer, smaller roots.
- Symptoms are clearest on young leaves.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)

### Cultural & Preventive Strategies
1. Plant only cuttings from symptom-free mother plants, ideally from certified clean multiplication sites.
2. Grow CMD-resistant varieties released for your region.
3. Rogue (pull out and destroy) plants that show symptoms within the first 2 to 3 months.
4. Avoid planting new fields next to older infected fields.

### Vector Control
- Insecticides against whiteflies rarely stop CMD on their own; clean planting material and resistance are the main tools.
- Where whitefly pressure is very high, neem-based products or approved systemic insecticides on young plants can reduce spread.
