# Agronomic Guide: Citrus Greening / Huanglongbing (Candidatus Liberibacter spp.)

## Overview & Pathology
Huanglongbing (HLB), or citrus greening, is caused by phloem-limited bacteria (*Candidatus* Liberibacter asiaticus and related species). It is spread by the Asian citrus psyllid (*Diaphorina citri*) and the African citrus psyllid (*Trioza erytreae*), and by grafting infected budwood. There is no cure; infected trees decline and become unproductive.

## Epidemiology
- Psyllids feed on new flush and carry the bacteria between trees.
- Symptoms can take months to years to appear, so infected trees spread the disease before they are noticed.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Keys
- **Blotchy mottle:** Asymmetric yellow patches across the leaf, not matching on both sides of the midrib. This is the key sign that separates HLB from nutrient deficiency.
- Yellow shoots, thickened and corky veins, upright small leaves.
- Small, lopsided fruit that stays green at the bottom, with bitter juice and aborted seeds.
- Twig dieback and heavy fruit drop.

## Integrated Disease Management (IDM)
1. Plant only certified disease-free nursery trees from protected (screened) nurseries.
2. Monitor and control psyllids on new flush, coordinated across neighbouring groves.
3. Remove and destroy infected trees promptly to reduce the source of bacteria.
4. Keep trees well-fed (balanced nutrition, micronutrients) to slow decline.
5. Report suspected HLB to the local plant protection service; it is a regulated disease in many countries.
