# Agronomic Guide: Two-Spotted Spider Mites (Tetranychus urticae)

## Overview & Biology
The two-spotted spider mite (*Tetranychus urticae*) is an extremely destructive polyphagous acarine pest affecting greenhouse vegetables, outdoor crops (corn, soybeans, strawberries), fruit trees, and ornamental plants. Due to their minute size (0.4-0.5 mm) and rapid reproductive rate, mite populations can escalate exponentially before casual visual detection.

## Environmental Triggers & Reproduction
- **Optimal Conditions:** Hot, dry, and dusty conditions accelerate spider mite development. At 30°C (86°F), an egg-to-adult generation completes in as few as 5 to 7 days.
- **Drought Stress:** Moisture-stressed host plants accumulate higher soluble nitrogen and sugars in foliage, creating an ideal nutrient source for accelerated mite fecundity.
- **Pesticide-Induced Flare-ups:** Broad-spectrum pyrethroid and organophosphate applications decimate beneficial predatory mites and insects, triggering devastating secondary mite outbreaks.

## Visual Symptoms & Diagnostic Signs
- **Foliar Stippling:** Puncturing of leaf epidermal cells produces fine white, bronze, or yellowish speckling (stippling) on the upper surface.
- **Bronzing & Desiccation:** Heavy infestations cause leaves to turn completely bronze, dry out, curl, and suffer premature defoliation.
- **Silk Webbing:** Silken webbing on the undersides of leaves, growing tips, and petiole junctions. Under peak populations, dense webs wrap entire shoot tips, allowing mites to "balloon" on air currents.
- **Field Confirmation Test:** Tap suspected leaves over a white sheet of paper; crawling specks the size of dust particles indicate active mites.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

### Cultural & Mechanical Management
1. **Dust Suppression & Irrigation:** Irrigate perimeter dirt roads and maintain adequate soil moisture. Overhead water sprinkler bursts can physically disrupt webs and dislodge nymphal colonies.
2. **Weed Reservoir Elimination:** Eradicate alternative host weeds (such as broadleaf dock, mallow, and bindweed) in field margins.
3. **Prompt Crop Residue Destruction:** Immediately chop and till or compost post-harvest crop residues to avoid mite migration to adjacent active fields.

### Biological Control (Beneficial Predators)
- **Predatory Mites:** Release of *Phytoseiulus persimilis* (effective in high humidity/greenhouses), *Neoseiulus californicus* (tolerant of high heat and drought), or *Amblyseius andersoni*.
- **Insect Predators:** Ladybird beetles (*Stethorus punctillum*), minute pirate bugs (*Orius insidiosus*), and predatory gall midges (*Feltiella acarisuga*).
- **Conserve Natural Enemies:** Avoid broad-spectrum neurotoxin insecticides to preserve endemic predatory mite colonies.

### Biorational & Chemical Miticides
- **Insecticidal Soaps & Horticultural Oils:** Potassium salts of fatty acids or light horticultural mineral oils (1-2% solution) suffocate mites on direct contact. Thorough under-leaf coverage is mandatory.
- **Botanical / Microbial Miticides:** Abamectin (derived from *Streptomyces avermitilis*), azadirachtin (neem), and entomopathogenic fungi (*Beauveria bassiana*).
- **Targeted Synthetic Miticides:** Bifenazate, Spiromesifen, Acequinocyl, or Hexythiazox. Rotate FRAC/IRAC modes of action (Groups 20, 21, 23, 10) to combat severe acaricide resistance.
